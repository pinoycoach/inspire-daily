```
Courage is resistance to fear, mastery of fear—not absence of fear. | Mark Twain | Pudd'nhead Wilson's Calendar, Pudd'nhead Wilson (1894) |
Well done is better than well said. | Benjamin Franklin | Poor Richard's Almanack (1737) |
Lost time is never found again. | Benjamin Franklin | Poor Richard's Almanack (1748) |
Dost thou love life? Then do not squander time, for that's the stuff life is made of. | Benjamin Franklin | Poor Richard's Almanack (1746) |
He that can have patience can have what he will. | Benjamin Franklin | Poor Richard's Almanack |
The best way out is always through. | Robert Frost | "A Servant to Servants", North of Boston (1914) |
I'm not afraid of storms, for I'm learning how to sail my ship. | Louisa May Alcott | Little Women, spoken by Amy |
Do what you can, with what you have, where you are. | Squire Bill Widener, as quoted by Theodore Roosevelt | Theodore Roosevelt: An Autobiography (1913) |
Nothing in the world is worth having or worth doing unless it means effort, pain, difficulty. | Theodore Roosevelt | Speech, Syracuse, New York (1903) |
It is not the man who has too little, but the man who craves more, that is poor. | Seneca | Letters to Lucilius, Letter 2 (trans. Richard M. Gummere, 1917) |
No longer talk at all about the kind of man that a good man ought to be, but be such. | Marcus Aurelius | Meditations, Book 10 (trans. George Long, 1862) |
We are what we repeatedly do. Excellence, then, is not an act, but a habit. | Will Durant, summarising Aristotle | The Story of Philosophy (1926) |
Fortune favors the bold. | Latin proverb | Found in Virgil, Aeneid, Book 10 ("audentes fortuna iuvat") |
"Hope" is the thing with feathers, that perches in the soul. | Emily Dickinson | Poem 314, published 1891 |
```

Notes to check:
- **Twain:** the original has the long dash. Keep it (exact quote) or use a comma (common version). Your call.
- **Franklin, 1748 and 1746:** years from memory [check against an Almanack edition].
- **Alcott:** in *Little Women* Part 2 (1869) as far as I recall [check the chapter].
- **Roosevelt 1903:** often dated to a Labor Day speech in Syracuse [check].
- **Durant:** this is the famous line wrongly credited to Aristotle. Durant's 1926 book is public domain in the US since 2022 [check for your market]. The credit above says it honestly, which is the point of the app.
- **Dickinson:** line breaks and punctuation differ between editions; the version above joins two lines [check].

**Avoid (popular but wrong):** "The secret of getting ahead is getting started" (not Twain); "Be the change you wish to see" (not Gandhi); "Keep your face to the sunshine" (not reliably Helen Keller); anything from living or modern authors (copyright).

## Filipino proverbs (salawikain), translations by Claude: **Tagalog proofread needed**

```
Ang taong nagigipit, sa patalim kumakapit. | Filipino proverb (salawikain) | | A person in desperation will grab even a blade.
Aanhin pa ang damo kung patay na ang kabayo. | Filipino proverb (salawikain) | | What use is the grass once the horse is dead?
Ang bayaning nasugatan, nag-iibayo ang tapang. | Filipino proverb (salawikain) | | A wounded hero grows twice as brave.
Ubos-ubos biyaya, bukas nakatunganga. | Filipino proverb (salawikain) | | Spend every blessing today, and tomorrow you sit staring.
Walang mahirap na gawa kapag dinaan sa tiyaga. | Filipino proverb (salawikain) | | No work is hard when you go at it with patience.
Ang pagsasabi ng tapat ay pagsasama nang maluwat. | Filipino proverb (salawikain) | | Telling the truth keeps people together for a long time.
Kapag may usok, may apoy. | Filipino proverb (salawikain) | | Where there is smoke, there is fire.
Nasa huli ang pagsisisi. | Filipino proverb (salawikain) | | Regret always comes at the end.
Tulak ng bibig, kabig ng dibdib. | Filipino proverb (salawikain) | | The mouth pushes away what the heart pulls close.
Ang taong walang kibo, nasa loob ang kulo. | Filipino proverb (salawikain) | | The quiet one is boiling inside.
Bato-bato sa langit, ang tamaan ay huwag magalit. | Filipino proverb (salawikain) | | A stone thrown at the sky: whoever it hits, don't get angry.
Matibay ang walis, palibhasa'y magkabigkis. | Filipino proverb (salawikain) | | The broom is strong because its strands are bound together.
Kapag binato ka ng bato, batuhin mo ng tinapay. | Filipino proverb (salawikain) | | If someone throws a stone at you, throw back bread.
Magbiro ka na sa lasing, huwag lang sa bagong gising. | Filipino proverb (salawikain) | | Joke with a drunk if you must, but never with someone who just woke up.
```

Notes:
- Spellings and wording vary by region and source; pick the version your readers know.
- **"Ang hindi marunong magmahal sa sariling wika…"** is left out on purpose: it is usually credited to Rizal, and historians doubt he wrote it.
- **Matibay ang walis** fits VA and team posts; **Ubos-ubos biyaya** fits sweldo week; **Kapag binato ka ng bato** fits Fearless Leadership.
